Self-reinforced preferential attachment

In joint work with Frank den Hollander, we introduced a preferential-attachment tree in which each new vertex chooses its attachment point according to the cumulative past degree of an existing vertex, rather than its degree at the current time alone. We established the almost-sure growth scale of the degree of each fixed vertex: the exponent is approximately 0.618, compared with 0.5 in classical linear preferential attachment. The proof combines stochastic approximation and martingale arguments. This model shows concretely how retaining the history of connectivity changes long-term growth.

With Frank den Hollander.

Interacting urns on graphs

With Neeraja Sahasrabudhe, I studied systems of interacting two-colour urns in which observations and reinforcement can depend on neighbouring urns in a graph. The models allow multiple drawings, with or without replacement, and include Pólya-type and Friedman-type rules. We proved almost-sure convergence and synchronisation under suitable assumptions, identifying how the graph and reinforcement mechanism influence the limits. In particular, certain bipartite Friedman models synchronise within each part of the graph but have complementary limits across the two parts. We also obtained central limit theorems in diffusive and critical regimes.

With Neeraja Sahasrabudhe.

Reinforcement and sampling on directed networks

With Gursharn Kaur and Neeraja Sahasrabudhe, I developed a general framework for interacting reinforced stochastic systems on directed networks. Agents may use different sampling rules, while reinforcement depends on the connections through which information is shared. The framework includes heterogeneous interacting urns and processes that use past observations, including urn representations of random walks with memory. We obtained graph-theoretic conditions for convergence to deterministic limits and, in complementary settings, convergence to random limits with synchronisation within suitable classes of agents. The analysis also gives rates of convergence, central limit theorems and laws of the iterated logarithm under appropriate assumptions.

With Gursharn Kaur and Neeraja Sahasrabudhe.

Monochromatic edges and graph-based tests

In joint work with Siva Athreya, Bhaswar B. Bhattacharya and collaborators, we investigated the number of monochromatic edges when vertices of a graph receive random colours. We derived third- and fourth-moment criteria for asymptotic normality and showed that, for certain colouring probabilities, convergence of the fourth moment alone is not enough to guarantee a Gaussian limit. The arguments combine graph-counting methods, Stein's method and Wasserstein bounds. We also applied the results to the asymptotic behaviour and consistency of nearest-neighbour graph-based two-sample tests in a Poissonized setting.

With Siva Athreya, Bhaswar B. Bhattacharya, Karambir Das, Spandan Ghoshal, Sourish Maniyar and Elizabeth Sara Roy.

Current research · Ashoka University

Directed random networks and the Brownian web/net

I am a Research Associate at Ashoka University on a CEFIPRA-supported Indo-French project concerned with directed random networks and their scaling limits. My current work includes studying the Brownian web, which describes collections of coalescing Brownian paths, and the Brownian net, which also allows branching. These continuum objects provide useful mathematical frameworks for understanding the large-scale behaviour of systems of paths.

Project team: Kumarjit Saha (Ashoka University), Anish Sarkar (ISI Delhi), David Coupier (IMT Nord Europe) and Viet Chi Tran (Université Gustave Eiffel).
